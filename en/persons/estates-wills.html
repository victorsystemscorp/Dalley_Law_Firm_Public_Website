The Problem

Without a Plan, the Court Decides Your Family's Future

If something happens to you without a will or plan, the state decides who handles your estate and who inherits — not you. Your family can face long court proceedings, disputes, and avoidable costs during the hardest time of their lives.

Planning ahead is one of the kindest things you can do. And it's far simpler than most people think.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need a will if I don&apos;t have much?+

Yes. A will names who handles your estate and who receives your belongings — and names guardians for minor children. Without one, the state decides, often with delay and family conflict.

What&apos;s the difference between a will and a trust?+

A will takes effect at death and goes through probate; a trust can hold assets during life and often avoids probate. We explain which fits your situation and budget.

What happens if a loved one died without a plan?+

We handle estate administration and guide the family through probate step by step — filing, inventory, distribution, and closing the estate properly.

Are estate plans expensive?+

Not with us. Many plans qualify for a fixed fee. The cost of not planning — court, conflict, and family strain — is almost always far higher.
